# Design: the dashboard redesign

Status: 2026-11-03. Direction chosen and mockups approved by Danny. Nothing built.

Mockups (Claude artifacts, private to Danny until shared):

- All screens in the chosen direction, plus the mascot sheets:
  https://claude.ai/artifact/8qCZB9NvLdVmrB5GhtRRHJ
- The three directions that were compared (A Calm, B Bleep, C Console):
  https://claude.ai/artifact/HUQSCtzBQCcbSS16aB8HiC

This document is the source of truth when the two disagree: the mockups use made-up incidents and
some copy that the API does not produce (see "Mockup copy that is not the contract").

## 1. Intent and why

Bleep's dashboard works, but it looks like every shadcn starter, and it reads as a list of panels
rather than an answer. Danny's reference for what to avoid is the latest Coolify update: lots on
screen, no overview. The redesign gives Bleep a face and a hierarchy.

When Telegram buzzes at night and Danny opens Bleep on his phone, the first second must answer, in
this order:

1. **What failed**, and whether Bleep needs him.
2. **How far it reaches**: other services too? the whole host?
3. **Everything else**, quietly, one click away.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**

- One visual language across all screens: overview, incident list, incident page (desktop and
  phone), report, service page, settings, developer panel.
- Bleep as a character: a small on-call bot whose mood and motion show the state of things, and
  who feels alive, not a picture that swaps.
- Light and dark mode on every screen; the incident page and its decision bar work one-handed on a
  phone.

**Non-goals**

- No change to the API, the data, the polling and streaming model, or any behaviour written down in
  `docs/design/app.md`. This is a restyle and a re-layout; every rule there still holds (first
  answer wins, buttons lock after an answer, the bar is bound to the approval id, etc.).
- No change to prompts or to the text the backend writes (diagnosis, plan reason, report). Bleep's
  first-person voice is only in copy the dashboard itself owns.
- No new pages beyond those the plan already has (#27 report, #30 service page, #41 settings,
  #42 developer panel). For Settings and the developer panel this document is the look only: #41 and
  #42 build those pages, with whatever API they need, in this style.
- No new API for the restyle itself. What a screen shows without data behind it today is listed in
  §11 with what to do instead.

**Anti-goals** (it would be a failure even if it worked)

- A screen that is full but tells you nothing (the Coolify problem).
- A mascot that replaces words: every state is still a word and an icon; colour and Bleep's face
  are extra cues, never the only one.
- Cute at the cost of exact: container names, ids, URLs and exit codes stay monospace and exact.
- Motion that distracts or that cannot be turned off.

## 3. Constraints

- Next.js app in `frontend/`, Tailwind and the existing component split. Data and logic stay where
  they are (`lib/`, the `*View` components that take props); the restyle should mostly touch markup
  and `globals.css`. #30 already kept its parts prop-driven for this reason.
- Health is never colour alone (`docs/design/app.md`, #9). Text contrast at least 4.5:1, touch
  targets at least 44 px.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` stops every animation.
- Fonts come through `next/font/google` like Geist does today.

## 4. Direction (decided)

Direction **B, "Bleep"**: friendly and rounded, with a mascot, chosen over A (calm, neutral) and C
(dense console). Soft lilac surfaces, pill-shaped controls, large radii, a rounded typeface, and the
same strict hierarchy as the other two directions.

## 6. Type

- **Nunito** for everything (400 to 800). Headlines 800, card titles 800 at 19 to 20 px, body 15 to
  17 px at 400 to 700. Page headline 32 to 36 px desktop, 22 px phone.
- **JetBrains Mono** (400, 500) for container names, ids, URLs, tool names and arguments, env
  variable names, prompt text. It is never bold.
- Section labels inside the report are small caps style: 13 px, 800, letter-spacing 0.06em,
  uppercase, `ink2`.

## 7. Bleep, the mascot

A small on-call bot: a rounded head with a dark visor for a face, a headset with a mic, a small body
with a status light on the chest, and two floating hands (no arms, no legs). The SVG source in the
mockup (`Bleep.dc.html` on the canvas) is the reference drawing; it uses a 120 × 120 viewBox:

- Head `rect 26,22 68×50 r24` in `blob`; visor `rect 34,33 52×26 r13` in `screen`.
- Headset: band `M24 44Q24 8 60 8T96 44` stroke `cup` 5; ear cups `rect 15,34 14×22 r7` and
  `rect 91,34 14×22 r7`; mic boom `M99 55q1 13-18 14`, ending in the **status light**
  (`circle 80,69 r4.5`).
- Body `rect 40,76 40×28 r12` in `blob` with a chest light `circle 60,90 r4` in the visor colour.
- Hands: round dots (stroke 13, `hand`), drawn **in front of** the head.

**Moods.** The visor eyes and the status light carry the state; the hands and motion add character.

| Mood | Eyes (visor colour) | Light | Hands | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All good | smiling arcs, `#7CF2B8` | `ok` | left low, right out to the side | breathes, blinks, waves twice now and then (3.6 s cycle), out to the side, never behind the head |
| Working | round, `#FFD36E` | `warn`, slow pulse | both low, floating | breathes; the eyes wander around the visor (left, right, up) with pauses |
| Needs you | big round, `#FF8E80` | `down`, fast blink | both raised | hops, waves both hands quickly to catch attention |
| Not sure | one squint, one round, `#C9C6F0` | `stop` | one scratching behind the ear cup | head tilts, a "?" floats above |
| Resting | smiling arcs | `ok` | both low, floating | breathes and blinks only |

Breathing is a 2.5 px vertical bob over 2.8 s; blinking squashes the eyes for a moment every 4.2 s.
All motion is CSS keyframes on SVG groups; with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` every animation is
off and Bleep stands still in its mood pose.

**Small sizes.** Below 40 px Bleep is the head only: no headset, no hands, no body. That is the
header logo (30 px) and the favicon (32, 22, 16 px). The logo's eyes take the overall state's colour
and still blink.

**Which mood where** (the order decides when several apply: Needs you, Not sure, Working, All good):

- Overview and header logo: any incident `awaiting_approval` or `failed` → Needs you; an awaiting
  incident whose diagnosis is `unsure` → Not sure; any incident `open`, `investigating`,
  `executing` or `verifying`, or an unreachable host → Working; otherwise All good.
- Incident page: `awaiting_approval` → Needs you (Not sure when `diagnosis.unsure`);
  `open` to `verifying` → Working; `resolved` → All good; `closed` → Resting; `failed` → Needs you.
- Report: `resolved` → All good; `unverified` and `budget` → Needs you; `rejected`, `no_action`,
  `replan_limit` → Resting.

**What the mood is computed from** (Claude's call): the header logo only needs a colour, so it uses
three states (red, amber, green) from one shared poll of `GET /api/incidents` (open statuses) plus
host reachability; it has no Not sure. The overview hero and the incident page may use Not sure,
because they read the incident detail: the overview fetches `GET /api/incidents/{id}` only for
incidents that wait for the human (normally none or one), on its existing 10 s poll. Both are reads
of existing endpoints, not API changes.

When the mood changes, the pose changes; the breathing never stops, so it reads as the same bot
reacting, not a new picture.

## 8. Components

- **App header**: head-only Bleep and "Bleep" as the home link; the nav as one pill group (Home,
  Incidents, Settings; the active item filled with `ink`); the theme toggle as a round icon button.
  The developer panel is not in the nav; Settings links to it when it is enabled.
- **Hero**: Bleep (132 px overview, 112 px incident, 72 px report, 76 px phone) beside a status tag,
  a headline and one line. It is the only place Bleep appears large.
- **Status pill**: icon + word on the status tint, fully round. Health words stay as today (OK,
  Degraded, Down, Stopped, Unknown); incident statuses as today except `awaiting_approval`, which
  reads "Needs your yes".
- **Card**: `surface`, 1 px `line`, large radius, title 19 to 20 px 800. The card that waits for
  the human (the plan awaiting a decision) gets a 2 px `accent` border.
- **Progress bar** (incident): eight segments Gather, Investigate, Diagnose, Plan, Your yes, Fix,
  Check, Report, driven by the node events the timeline already reads; done `ok`, current `accent`
  and bold label, to do `line`. Fix and Check stay grey when nothing ran (rejected, no action); a
  `failed` incident shows the step it stopped at in `down`; a `closed` incident shows Report done.
- **Plan step**: numbered round badge, verb in bold, target in mono, a short why; while running the
  badge becomes a spinner, then a check.
- **Decision bar**: "Yes, go ahead" (primary), "No, leave it", "Tell Bleep something". On a phone it
  is pinned to the bottom of the screen: the primary button full width at 56 px, the other two side
  by side at 48 px; after an answer the bar becomes one status line (running, checking, fixed and
  checked, or "You said no. Nothing ran."). Every rule of today's `DecisionBar` stays (locking, 409
  message, keyed by approval id, 2,000 character instruction).
- **Tool call row** ("How I got there"): a plain sentence, the tool name and time in mono on the
  right, the result's first line folded under it.
- **Timeline**: a coloured dot per entry (red for the trigger and "Waiting for you", amber while
  running, `accent` for the human's answer, green for resolved), text, time in mono.
- **Feedback buttons**: round, icon + word, pressed state in the matching tint.
- **Filter chips**, **segmented control**, **switch**, **text field** (radius 14, 44 px tall),
  **secret row** (name, env name in mono, "Set" or "Missing").

## 9. Screens

**Overview** (`/`). Hero first: Bleep in the overall mood and a headline that answers "what failed"
("shop is down. I know why, and I have a fix.") with the plan in one line and "Show me the plan".
With nothing wrong: "Nothing needs you right now." With an unreachable host, the host is the
headline. Then "Your hosts" as tiles, one per host, with its services as pill rows (health icon,
name, word) and its basics in mono; the line above the tiles says how far it reaches ("Only Demo
stack is hurting. The other two are fine."). A host with a problem gets the `down` tint and border.
Then "Fixed themselves" (recoveries). A service name links to its page.

**Incident list** (`/incidents`). Headline with counts, a search box, filter chips (All, Needs you,
Open, Resolved, Closed or failed). Groups: "Needs you", "I am on it", "Earlier". Each row: status
pill, title, one line on the outcome, subject in mono, time and duration. A row waiting for the human
has the `down` border. An empty list says so in one dashed card.

**Incident page** (`/incidents/[id]`). Hero with Bleep in the incident's mood, a headline for the
moment ("shop is down. Can I fix it?", "On it.", "Almost there.", "Fixed and checked."), the subject,
time and cost, and the status pill. Below, the progress bar. Main column: the report when the run has
written one (as today, it sits on top), the plan card with the decision bar, "What I found" (the
diagnosis with confidence and category pills, the evidence as plain lines, the unsure banner as
today, a "Seen before" block when memory recalled a similar incident), and "How I got there". Side
column: the timeline. While the plan runs, the plan card shows each step's progress, then the
verification checks as pills.

**Incident on a phone.** One column: a small header (back, subject, theme), Bleep at 76 px with the
status pill and the headline, the report when present, "What I found" in two sentences, the plan, and
"How I got there" folded. The decision bar is pinned to the bottom (see Components). Nothing else
competes with it.

**Report.** The content of today's `ReportView` in a document card: Bleep in the outcome's mood, the
outcome pill, the title, the subject, times and cost in mono, then "In short", "Why it happened",
"What I did" (each action with a check, a cross or a dash), "What you can do" when the plan was no
action (the human instructions, in a `soft` block), "Your decision", "Timeline" and "Follow-ups".
"Was this right?" with Right and Wrong sits on the diagnosis and the plan as today; the mockup also
shows it under the report. Outcomes: `resolved` green, `unverified` and `budget` amber, the rest
grey, as in `docs/design/app.md`. It must print cleanly (navigation hidden).

**Service page** (`/services/[id]`). Breadcrumb, the service name as headline with its health pill
and one line (source, host, container count, healthy since). Cards: "What I know about shop" (the
facts with their source as today: "Added by you" or "Learned from an incident's report" with a link;
delete per fact; an add box), "Checked from outside" (each URL with its last status and latency, and
a strip of the last checks as green and red ticks), "Containers", "Rules I follow" (the service's
and its host's, plus "I never change anything without your yes"), and "Past incidents" with the
human's feedback per incident. The line for a service that is gone from its host stays.

**Settings** (#41). A side list of sections (Hosts, Checks, URLs, Rules, Telegram, Models, Plugins,
Secrets) that stacks on a phone. Hosts are read-only, marked "From config/hosts.yaml". Validation
errors from the API show under the field, in `down`, with the field's border in `down`, and the save
bar says how many fields need fixing. Telegram shows Connected, the masked chat id and the last poll.
Models is a segmented OpenRouter / Local switch. Plugins are cards with a switch and their tools as
chips labelled read (green) or act (amber). Secrets show only Set or Missing with the env name; no
value ever appears.

**Developer panel** (#42). Reached from Settings, marked with a `BLEEP_DEV_PANEL=true` pill in the
header. Cards: Spend (today against the daily cap as a bar, the last seven days as bars, cost per
incident), Budgets and model (the fields of `schemas/settings.py`), Prompts (the list of prompt files
with an "override" tag, an editor in mono, the placeholder check under it in green or red, Save
override and Reset to file), Guard log (filter chips by kind, a table of time, kind, incident and
what happened).

## 10. Voice

- Copy the dashboard owns speaks as Bleep, in first person and plain words: "I know why, and I have a
  fix", "I am on it", "How I got there", "What I know about shop", "Rules I follow".
- Data stays neutral and exact: labels, pills, table headers, tool names, numbers.
- Text written by the backend (root cause, evidence, plan reason, report sections, recovery lines) is
  shown as it comes. Changing its voice is a prompt change with its own evals, not part of this.

## 11. Mockup copy that is not the contract

The mockups invent some things to look real. They are not requirements:

- The incidents, hosts (`coolify-main`, `coolify-sites`), costs and timings.
- The report written in first person; the real report text comes from the Report node.
- "Your instruction on Telegram" as a fact's source; the API has `human` and `report` only.
- Things with no data behind them today. The rule for all of them (Claude's call): show it when the
  API provides it, leave it out until then, and never add an API just for the restyle; a later issue
  may add the data.
  - The per-incident feedback summary on the service page ("You: diagnosis right").
  - "Seen before" on the incident page: `IncidentDetail` does not carry the recalled incidents.
  - The strip of past URL checks on the service page: `UrlStatus` has only the latest check, so show
    that one.
  - "Healthy since" on the service page.
  - The one-line outcome on each incident-list row: `Incident` has no summary; the row shows title,
    status, subject and time.
  - The plan in one line on the overview hero comes from the waiting incident's detail (§7); without
    it the hero shows the incident's title and "Show me the plan".
- The mockup's settings fields (grace, thresholds, plugins, secrets) follow the issues' "Done when"
  lists; #41 decides which ones exist.

## 12. Left to the builder

- How to split the work into issues and PRs (a sensible order: tokens, fonts and the header; the
  mascot component; then one screen per PR).
- The component and file names, and whether the mascot is one component with a `mood` and `size`
  prop (recommended) or several.
- Exact spacing between the values given above, icon choices (lucide stays fine), and the motion
  curves within the durations given.
- Whether the report keeps its place on top of the incident page only or also gets a printable
  route.

## 13. Calls made in the mockups (Claude's, not Danny's)

These were not discussed; they are easy to change:

- "Overview" is renamed "Home" in the nav.
- `awaiting_approval` reads "Needs your yes"; the approve button reads "Yes, go ahead" and reject
  "No, leave it".
- Healthy hosts show their services in the tile (there are few); a host with a problem is tinted.
- The phone screen starts dark and most desktop screens light in the mockups only to show both
  themes; the app keeps today's system / light / dark choice.
- Bleep's mood order (Needs you over Not sure over Working over All good) and the mapping in §7.
